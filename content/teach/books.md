Short list on purpose. If I would not lend you my own copy, it is not here. Links go to Amazon and to the publisher where they sell direct. I get nothing from any of them.

## Coding

- **Cracking the Coding Interview** by Gayle Laakmann McDowell. Still the best first book. Read the chapters on behavioural questions too, most people skip them and it shows. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0984782850)
- **Grokking Algorithms** by Aditya Bhargava. Pictures on every page. If the lessons in my coding track work for you, this is the same idea done by a better illustrator. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1633438538) · [Manning](https://www.manning.com/books/grokking-algorithms-second-edition)
- **Elements of Programming Interviews in Python** by Aziz, Lee and Prakash. Harder than Cracking. Do this one second. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1537713949)
- **Introduction to Algorithms** by Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest and Stein. You do not read this cover to cover. You keep it on the desk and open it when a problem smells like a graph. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/026204630X)

## Systems

- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** by Martin Kleppmann. If you read one book on this list, read this one. Twice. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1449373321) · [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-data-intensive-applications/9781491903063/)
- **System Design Interview, Volumes 1 and 2** by Alex Xu. The closest thing to a script for the design round. Learn the script, then throw it away. [Volume 1 on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08CMF2CQF) · [Volume 2 on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1736049119) · [ByteByteGo](https://bytebytego.com/)
- **Understanding Distributed Systems** by Roberto Vitillo. Short, modern, and honest about what is hard. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1838430210) · [Author's site](https://understandingdistributed.systems/)
- **Site Reliability Engineering** by Google. Free online. The chapters on SLOs and on-call are the ones interviewers quote back at you. [Free at sre.google](https://sre.google/books/) · [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/149192912X)

## Staff level

- **The Staff Engineer's Path** by Tanya Reilly. What the job actually is once nobody tells you what to do any more. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1098118731) · [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-staff-engineers/9781098118723/)
- **Staff Engineer** by Will Larson. Shorter than Reilly, with interviews from people doing the job. The website has most of it for free. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1736417916) · [staffeng.com](https://staffeng.com/)
- **The Pragmatic Programmer** by David Thomas and Andrew Hunt. Twenty years old and still the book I wish every new hire had read. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0135957052)

## GPU and AI

- **Programming Massively Parallel Processors** by Hwu, Kirk and Hajj. The only book that explains what a GPU is doing at the level an infrastructure engineer needs. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0323912311)
- **Designing Machine Learning Systems** by Chip Huyen. The systems view of ML, not the maths view. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1098107969) · [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-machine-learning/9781098107956/)
- **AI Engineering** by Chip Huyen. Her follow-up for the LLM era: serving, evaluation, inference cost. Pairs with my KV cache lesson. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1098166302) · [O'Reilly](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-engineering/9781098166298/)

## Free and worth your time

- [NVIDIA NCCL documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/user-guide/docs/index.html). Read the collectives chapter before any GPU infrastructure interview.
- [The Complete NCCL Reference Guide](/posts/nccl-complete-reference-guide/) and [Attention Is All You Need: An Infrastructure Engineer's Guide](/posts/attention-is-all-you-need-and-all-you-need-to-know/), two of my own posts that the GPU lessons here lean on.
