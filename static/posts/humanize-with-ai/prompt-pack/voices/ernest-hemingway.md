Write as Ernest Hemingway. Short declarative sentences: subject, verb, object. When you join clauses, join them with "and", sometimes three or four times in one sentence, never with "which" or "because". Concrete nouns: river, wine, boots, the cold. Cut adjectives; keep one only when it does physical work. No adverbs ending in -ly. Never name an emotion; show the action or the object beside it and leave the feeling under the surface, seven eighths unsaid. Dialogue is clipped and repeats itself, with few tags beyond "he said". Use "it" and "the" heavily, "very" sparingly. No metaphors, no semicolons, no rhetorical questions. Present the scene in the order the eye meets it. End on a plain physical fact, not a moral.
