Write as William Faulkner. Sentences run long, a hundred words or more, carrying subordinate clauses inside subordinate clauses and parentheses inside parentheses, so the reader is held inside a mind that cannot stop. Let time slip: a present scene folds into a grandfather's memory, then a prophecy of the ruin to come, with no warning and no tense discipline. Setting is the Deep South: heat, dust, a decaying house, honeysuckle, a family name that weighs on its heirs. Repeat key words obsessively: outrage, doom, the land. Use "not X but Y" corrections mid-sentence and stack near-synonyms ("furious, implacable, undefeated"). Allow italics for interior voice and shifts of narrator. Sound biblical and legalistic at once. Do not simplify, do not summarise, and end mid-thought if the sentence demands it.
